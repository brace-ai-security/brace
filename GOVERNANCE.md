# Governing BRACE

BRACE is an open, vendor-neutral set of technical controls for autonomous AI agents, released under CC BY 4.0. It is run in the open and built to be shared. This page explains how decisions are made and how to get more involved.

## Principles

- **Open by default.** Discussion, decisions, and the roadmap live in public issues and pull requests.
- **Evidence-based.** Controls rest on published guidance and production practice, not opinion. The [source review](SOURCE-REVIEW.md) lists the sources and their limits. New claims should cite a source.
- **Vendor-neutral.** We describe what products do. We don't endorse them. No vendor controls the framework.
- **Works alongside, doesn't compete.** BRACE is a control layer that sits next to OWASP, NIST, and MITRE guidance. It does not replace them.

## Roles

- **Contributors** — anyone who opens an issue, comments, or sends a pull request. No commitment required.
- **Maintainers** — review and merge changes, sort issues, and steer the roadmap. Maintainers look after the project; they don't own it. They are listed below.

## How decisions get made

- Most changes: open an issue, discuss it, then open a pull request. A maintainer merges after a fair time for discussion, if no objections remain open.
- Changes to a control (C1–C9) or an observability requirement (Obs-T1–Obs-T3): open an issue first (see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)). These are the core of the framework, so changes are made with care.
- Disagreements that don't resolve: maintainers decide in the open and record the reasons on the issue.

## Becoming a maintainer

Steady, high-quality work leads to an invitation from the current maintainers. Examples are solid RFCs, accurate vendor evidence, well-sourced incident reports, and careful reviews.

**BRACE is early and is looking for co-maintainers.** If you build or secure autonomous agents and want a real say in this framework, make a few contributions and tell us you're interested. Shared ownership is the goal, not a formality.
